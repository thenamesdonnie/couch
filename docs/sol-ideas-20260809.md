1. **Self-Healing Launches** — **WEEKEND**  
   Learn the observable signature of a successful launch for every game: expected processes, windows, focus changes and frames. When launchers, dialogs or black screens derail it, couchd executes a proven recovery path; new recoveries remain shadow proposals until validated.

2. **Input Firewall** — **WEEKEND**  
   Detect stick drift, stuck triggers, duplicated Bluetooth events and “button released while disconnected” failures before games receive them. Stage 2 can quarantine only the defective control, synthesize a neutral state, and show a precise diagnosis on the TV or phone.

3. **What Just Happened?** — **WEEKEND**  
   A button opens a causal explanation of the last few seconds: “Steam stole focus after opening this window; couchd restored the game 380 ms later.” The permanent, timestamped corpus makes this an actual trace explanation rather than a guessed status screen.

4. **Verified Ghost Hands** — **PROJECT**  
   Record brittle interaction sequences—launcher dismissal, mod-profile selection, accessibility setup—then replay them against visual and process checkpoints rather than fixed delays. If reality diverges, injection stops and rolls back instead of blindly continuing.

5. **Universal Soft Keyboard Layer** — **PROJECT**  
   Detect text-entry states from frames plus input-response evidence, then temporarily reinterpret the pad as a console-grade radial or predictive keyboard drawn by the compositor. Unlike phone typing, it works locally inside games that expose no text API and disappears only after verified submission or cancellation.

6. **Accessibility Autopilot** — **PROJECT**  
   Identify repeated execution barriers such as rapid taps, long holds, narrow timing windows or simultaneous inputs, then propose narrowly scoped assistance for that exact interaction. Input ownership supplies the transformation; shadow mode proves it does not trigger during ordinary play before activation is offered.

7. **Semantic Freeze** — **PROJECT**  
   Zero-cost pause becomes more than `SIGSTOP`: couchd classifies whether suspension occurred during dialogue, a timed choice, matchmaking, saving, a cutscene or ordinary play. The shell can then preserve the relevant context—dialogue transcript, remaining choice time, network-risk warning—without pretending every frozen frame is equally resumable.

8. **Household Interruptibility Oracle** — **PROJECT**  
   Learn which observed states are safe interruption points: loading screens, menus, post-match flows, credits, quiet playback boundaries. TV toasts, door alerts or remote requests can queue until the next predicted low-cost moment, with delivery policy trained and shadow-scored from actual dismissals and interruptions.

9. **Cross-Application Control Grammar** — **PROJECT**  
   Infer semantic equivalences from outcomes: which input means back, confirm, pause, skip, dismiss or reveal controls in each application—even when mappings and UI frameworks differ. The shell exposes one consistent couch vocabulary, while per-application translations graduate only after their effects are repeatedly verified.

10. **Interactive Failure Capsules** — **PROJECT**  
    When something breaks, package the preceding input stream, process/window timeline, relevant logs, screenshots and couchd causal trace into a scrub-able incident capsule. A developer can replay the orchestration around the failure, branch at any decision, and test a replacement policy against the recorded reality.

11. **Counterfactual Console Lab** — **RESEARCH**  
    Run candidate policies over months of corpus data to ask concrete questions: “Would this focus rule have fixed more incidents than it created?” Combine logged effects with learned state transitions to rank automations by expected benefit and surface the exact historical cases where they disagree with production.

12. **UI Cartographer** — **RESEARCH**  
    Build per-game maps of menus and states by correlating frames, injected probes and verified transitions, producing a navigable graph without game integration. This could power commands like “take me to controller settings” or “return to the title screen,” with execution constrained to previously proven routes.
