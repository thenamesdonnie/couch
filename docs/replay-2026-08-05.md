## Method, and what the replay can and cannot do

`tools/shadow-diff` compares two recorded streams; it has no model-replay
mode. So the replay was built per the mandate's fallback: reconstruct
`Observed` from the corpus's `kind:obs` records and drive the **real**
`Machine` + `reconcile()` over them, the way `test_reconcile.py`'s `Rig`
does. The replayed intents then replace the recorded ones in a synthetic
shadow log, which is fed to the unmodified differ — so the matching,
cooldown-association and triage machinery is exactly the production one.

Harness: `scratchpad/replay.py` (not committed; it is a throwaway, and the
committed successor should be a `--replay` flag on shadow-diff — see
*For the daytime crew*).

**Fidelity, measured not assumed.** Replaying the *Evening-1* model over the
Evening-1 corpus should reproduce the Evening-1 stream. It does:

* every one of the 31 recorded `(verb, subject, reason)` classes reappears,
  and the replay invents no class the daemon did not emit;
* totals 187 replayed vs 172 recorded, absolute per-class error 39;
* the gesture families match within ±1 (`freeze/set_flag/snapshot
  gesture:ps-hold` 14 vs 13, `route_pad/show/close_steam_menu
  gesture:hold-release` 14 vs 15);
* the residue is on level-based repair classes (`guard:wanted-window-not-on-top`
  10 vs 7, `thaw reconcile:frozen-without-suspended-flag` 7 vs 4), where a
  cooldown boundary landing one pass earlier or later changes the count.

Pass cadence is emulated from the daemon's own rule (0.2s inside a 3s
attention window opened by any observed change or transition, 5s otherwise),
because getting that wrong is the single biggest artifact generator: an early
version sampling at 0.05s inflated the repair classes by 60%.

**Four `Observed` fields are not in the Evening-1 corpus at all**, and the
replay's treatment of each is a caveat on any conclusion that depends on it:

| field | why it is missing | replay does |
|---|---|---|
| `kodi_window_present`, `big_picture_window` | the x11 observer emitted **zero** obs records all evening — that is exactly the bug 19085a7 fixed | inferred from the window stack the `world` obs carries; `big_picture_window` only detectable while BP is on top |
| `guide_consumed_at` | the corpus counts guide *lines* (SENT + SKIPPED together); the SENT/SKIPPED split that R7(f) turns on was only added tonight | `None` — so R7(f) **cannot fire** in the main replay (see sensitivity run below) |
| `launcher_alive` | never logged | `True` when a pid tree exists, else unknown. Harmless here: the `session:orphaned` state is never reached in this corpus |
| `guard_pid_ours` | couchd wrote no pidfile in shadow | `False`, which is correct for Evening 1 |

**The replay is open-loop.** Replayed intents are never applied to the
recorded world. Any repair that would have converged the world on its first
emission instead re-fires every cooldown for as long as the recorded
condition persisted. COUCHD-ONLY counts for repair classes are therefore
**upper bounds on emissions, not counts of decisions**. This is the whole
explanation for 57 of the 98 new COUCHD-ONLY rows (T1 below).

## Remaining divergences, triaged

### T4 — bug in couchd (the gating bucket). Three, all new.

**T4-1. `close_steam_menu` is emitted, and would be PERFORMED,
unconditionally on every hold-release handoff. This gates the `gestures`
flip.**

`_handoff_intents()` (couchd.py:1133) appends `close_steam_menu` with no
condition at all. The acting side is `_a_close_steam_menu` →
`self.act.spawn(None, shell_line=GUIDE_PRESS_SH)` (couchd.py:2266) — it
sends the guide press without ever checking whether Steam's menu is open.

The legacy stack does **not** do this. `handoff_to_kodi()` spawns
`steam-input-guard kodi`, and the guard's invariant 1 checks the routing log
for `ClientUI` before pressing. That is why the two streams look like this:

* couchd emits `close_steam_menu` **21 times** in Evening 1 (19 at
  `gesture:hold-release`, 2 at `gesture:hold-release-timeout`);
* legacy emits it **11 times** (4 `ClientUI routed but steam not focused`,
  7 `desktop-overlay-after-tap`);
* exactly **1** of them matches in time.

The guide button is a toggle — couchd's own `TOGGLE_VERBS` comment says so,
and commit f2ac188's latch exists because of it. So with `gestures` owned by
couchd, roughly **10 of every 21 hold-releases would OPEN the Steam menu over
Kodi**, on precisely the gesture whose purpose is to give the room back to
Kodi. In shadow this costs nothing, which is why it has sat UNTRIAGED in the
COUCHD-ONLY column since Evening 1 (it is 27 of the 48 recorded COUCHD-ONLY
rows) without anyone reading it as a bug.

Fix shape: gate the intent on `o.steam_menu_open` (the predicate already
exists on `Observed`), or gate the actuator, or both. It cannot be left to
the cooldown — the cooldown stops a *second* press, not a first one into a
closed menu.

**T4-2. The supersede-kill can SIGTERM a stale, possibly recycled pid.**
The 3 SET-MISMATCH rows.

`_supersede_guard_intent()` takes `o.guard_pid` straight from the pidfile
observation and `_a_kill` is a bare `os.kill(pid, SIGTERM)` with no liveness
or identity check (`Actuators.signal`, couchd.py:1752).

At 02:21:52.392 couchd names guard pid **1673365**, but the pidfile had
already been rewritten to **1673613** at 02:21:51.980 — 412ms earlier.
During that stretch the guard respawned five times in twenty seconds
(1673365 → 1673613 → 1673709 → 1673792 → 1674075). couchd now decides on the
gesture edge, ~50ms after BTN_MODE, which makes it *more* likely to read a
pidfile mid-rewrite than the old 5s tick was.

In shadow this is free. With `enforcement` owned it is a SIGTERM aimed at a
pid that has already exited — and on a box spawning processes this fast, at a
pid that may have been reused. The existing guard rail (`guard_pid_ours`)
protects couchd's own pidfile and nothing else.

Fix shape: re-read the pidfile at execute time, and verify the pid is alive
and is a `steam-input-guard` before signalling. `owned_resources()` already
does the psutil liveness check — the actuator should use the same one.

Note the differ paired these rows only because verb and subject match: they
are genuinely *different decisions* (legacy `superseded-by-newer-guard`,
couchd `superseded-by-freeze`). The `reason` mismatch is visible in the
ARGS-DIFF flag on all three rows.

**T4-3. The two halves of the refreeze repair have cooldowns 6× apart.**

`reconcile:refreeze-lost-suspend` emits a `freeze` (no explicit cooldown →
default **30s**) and an `_iconify_intent` (cooldown **5s**). Over the one
long episode they fired **9 freezes and 48 iconifies** — exactly the 5.3:1
the cooldowns predict. Post-flip that is six window unmaps per freeze. It is
idempotent so nothing breaks, but it is log noise, it makes C17's
predicted-effect bookkeeping report five satisfied-on-arrival iconifies per
repair, and it means the pair no longer behaves as one decision. The same
asymmetry exists on the handoff path, where `_handoff_intents`' route/show
are 3s and its iconify is 5s.

### T1 — harness / replay artifact. 57 rows, and the reason COUCHD-ONLY tripled.

`iconify 367520 reconcile:refreeze-lost-suspend` ×48 and `freeze` ×9, all in
**one episode, 02:13:38 – 02:17:53 (255s)**. The console genuinely sat with
`/tmp/game-suspended` set and the game's processes running, with neither
stack repairing it, until the operator resumed at 02:17:53 — this is the
5 Aug race that motivated the R7(d) catch-up in the first place. couchd's new
model is **right** to repair it. It emits 57 times only because the replay is
open-loop: nothing applies the freeze, so the condition never clears. Live
and acting, the first freeze converges the world and the repair stops.

The count is an artifact; the *decision* is a genuine improvement (T5) and
the underlying state is a genuine legacy bug (T3).

## The latency story — what this replay does NOT prove

**State this plainly in the morning summary.** The edge instrumentation
(`kind:'latency'`, `event:'gesture-edge'`) landed with 19085a7. The
Evening-1 corpus predates it. `shadow-diff`'s new `edge_latency()` summary
therefore returns **None for all three runs** — recorded, old replay and new
replay alike. **There is no Evening-1 measurement of couchd's edge-to-decision
latency, and the replay cannot manufacture one.**

The p95 the mandate asks to show is the **matched-offset** distribution,
which is a different quantity: the time between couchd's decision and the
legacy stack's decision for the same thing. It is an *inter-stack offset*,
not couchd's reaction time.

Baseline (Evening-1 corpus, Evening-1 differ, reproduced exactly):

```
  matched-offset ms (min/p50/p95/max), bound 250ms for route_pad/show
    route_pad   n=29    2   156   479    595
    show        n=26    5   156  1445   1526      <-- the 1445ms
    set_flag    n=11   13   162  3830   7446
  FAIL show matched-offset p95 <= 250ms      (p95=1445ms over 26 matches)
  FAIL route_pad matched-offset p95 <= 250ms (p95=479ms over 29 matches)
```

Under the edge-driven replay, the **medians** improve markedly and the
**p95s do not**:

| verb | p50 old → new | p95 old → new |
|---|---|---|
| `route_pad` | 159 → **49** ms | 477 → 458 ms |
| `show` | 150 → **100** ms | 1443 → 1459 ms |
| `snapshot` | 135 → **41** ms | 202 → 126 ms |
| `freeze` | 134 → **51** ms | 203 → 291 ms |
| `show_switcher` | 54 → 102 ms | 93 → **149** ms (n 2 → 7) |

Read that carefully and do not overclaim it:

1. The p50 improvement is **simulated**, not measured. It comes from my
   replay scheduling a pass 50ms after each BTN_MODE record. It shows the
   edge rule *would* move the median decision inside the perceptual bound; it
   is not evidence that the shipped implementation does.
2. The p95 barely moves because the tail is not couchd's — it is dominated by
   pairs where the *legacy* side is late, and by 17 additional matches the
   new model created (`show` n rose 26 → 44), which drag new tail in.
3. `show p95 = 1445ms` is therefore **not** "couchd took 1.4 seconds". It is
   "on the slowest 5% of shared show decisions, the two stacks were 1.4s
   apart". The real successor number is `edge_latency`, and it starts
   accumulating on Evening 2.

One genuine ordering improvement, from real recorded timestamps rather than
simulated ones: the `route_pad(kodi) after gesture release` assertion goes
from 2 failures / 37 passes (old replay) to **1 failure / 45 passes**, and
the surviving failure is on the weaker gesture-transition fallback channel,
not the kernel-timestamp channel. `freeze before set_flag(suspended)` passes
42/42.

## What Evening 2 still has to cover (C9)

The transition table has **94 state-changing edges. 38 were never exercised**
across the whole Evening-1 corpus plus its replay. The ones that matter:

**Blocking for a flip — these are the failure paths:**

* every `* → unknown` edge in every region (`x-unreachable`,
  `kodi-unreadable`, `flags-unreadable`, `pad-observer-blind`) — **11 edges,
  zero coverage**. couchd's entire R4 story is "an unknown region suppresses
  the intents that depend on it", and no evening has yet made a region
  unknown. This is what the staged fault rig is for; it is a daytime job and
  it is now the highest-value one on the list.
* `session: active → orphaned` (`launcher-gone-no-game`) and
  `orphaned → none`. Audit failure 8, never seen live.
* `enforcement: game → kodi` (`guard-window-superseded`).
* `input_ownership: kodi → steam-menu` (`steam-holds-the-pad`) and
  `steam-menu → kodi`. Steam grabbing the pad is the case invariant 1 exists
  for, and the region path into it was never walked.

**Gesture vocabulary gaps:**

* `long-hold-fired` is unreachable in every direction — `long_hold` is bound
  to `none`, so three edges have never run.
* `down-again → tap-resume` (a second tap that turns out to be a resume).
* `hold-fired → idle` (`released_no_handoff`): a hold whose action is not
  `suspend_to_kodi`.
* `unknown → down`: a press arriving before the gesture region is known.

**Foreground / Big Picture:**

* `bigpicture → kodi`, `bigpicture → other`, `game → bigpicture`,
  `unknown → bigpicture`. Big Picture was on screen for only 16 world samples
  all evening, and this is precisely where the x11 observer was blind. With
  x11 now emitting, a BP pass on Evening 2 is worth more than it was.

Plus the two catch-ups that could not be tested at all: **R7(f)** needs a
tap-resume on a game in Steam *desktop* UI mode with the new SENT/SKIPPED
instrumentation live, and **R7(g)** needs the differ taught to diff `launch`.
